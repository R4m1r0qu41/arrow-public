## 3. Canonical Message Standard at Genesis

The initial Arrow structure was defined as a fixed six-field canonical string:

```
Class:Origin:State:Policy:Finality:Confidence
```

This compact form encoded:

- **Class** — what happened
- **Origin** — where the assertion came from
- **State** — the current condition of the event
- **Policy** — the allowed downstream action or constraint
- **Finality** — whether the state was considered final
- **Confidence** — the trust level attached to the assertion

---

## 4. Problem Statement at Genesis

The Arrow Protocol originated from the recognition that existing systems lack a unified, deterministic way to express financial and digital asset state across heterogeneous environments. Messaging standards often lack semantic enforcement, smart contracts often lack interoperable event semantics across systems, and off-chain workflows frequently depend on ad hoc flags, inconsistent schemas, and implicit assumptions.

The core problem identified at genesis: **the absence of a standardized, machine-verifiable language for expressing state, intent, finality, and trust across blockchain and off-chain systems.**

---

## 5. Foundational Design Principles

The protocol was designed from first principles around six constraints:

- **Determinism** — every message should be parsed identically across systems.
- **Minimal surface area** — compact encoding with controlled vocabularies.
- **Semantic enforcement** — valid structure does not automatically imply valid meaning.
- **Lifecycle integrity** — events must form an append-only, coherent chain.
- **Trust encoding** — every event must carry an explicit confidence layer.
- **System-agnostic interoperability** — the protocol should not be tied to a single chain, institution, or jurisdiction.

## 9. Intellectual Positioning

**Arrow Protocol is a stateful event coordination standard with embedded legal semantics.**

This is a precise classification, not a provisional one. Three design decisions at genesis make it specific:

- The `LEG` origin code designates legal systems as first-class asserting domains alongside blockchains and custodians.
- The `PROTOCOL` jurisdiction value handles cross-border, arbitration-governed, and smart contract-native legal instruments where no single geographic jurisdiction applies.
- The `jurisdiction_model` in the Origin registry makes legal semantics machine-readable, not merely documentary.

This combination distinguishes Arrow from generic blockchain interoperability standards such as IBC (Inter-Blockchain Communication) or CCIP (Cross-Chain Interoperability Protocol), which treat legal systems as out of scope. Arrow treats legal systems as a native assertion domain on equal footing with on-chain origins. The legal enforceability layer listed in Future Extensions is a direct extension of this foundation, not an afterthought.
